TALKING SCRIPTS

Review-2 script (2 minutes)
We are Team 05. SonicAtlas asks how the sound of music changes across genres and eras. We cleaned about 1.2 million public Spotify tracks. Some tracks had a tempo of zero, which is impossible, so we repaired them with MICE. To test MICE we hid 5 percent of known values, and its error was up to 57 percent lower than simply filling the average. We flagged outliers and re-releases instead of deleting them. Our statistics follow our course: n minus 1 variance, Tukey quartiles, G1 and G2. Energy rises with loudness and falls with acousticness. PCA, t-SNE and LLE show that genres overlap, and t-SNE keeps local structure best. Next we finish the website, the lyrics and network analysis, and the paper.

Review-3 script (2 minutes)
Since Review-2 we finished everything. Music became about 7.8 decibels louder, with change points in 1989 and 1999, the CD loudness-war years. Genre explains 42% of the variation in danceability. Lyric mood barely follows musical mood. The website runs SQL inside the browser with DuckDB, so the data stays private and hosting is free. It passes 65 unit tests and 8 end-to-end tests, answers a query in a few milliseconds, and scores 98 to 100 in Lighthouse. The IEEE paper is prepared for submission.

Final script (2 minutes)
Open music data is messy and usually shown in static charts. We built a documented pipeline over 1.2 million tracks, 114 genres and 161 thousand English lyrics. We used the course methods: descriptive statistics, correlation, PCA, SVD, t-SNE, LLE, STL, change points, ANOVA and chi-square. We delivered a serverless website where anyone can query, map and compare the data. Our five findings: music got louder and less acoustic; energy follows loudness; genre explains 42 percent of danceability; t-SNE is the best map; and lyric sentiment is almost independent of audio valence. Our limits: Spotify features are model-made, the catalogue favours recent years, only 10.7% of the genre data joins to the audio table, and lyrics are English only.
